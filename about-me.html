ABOUT / ZAC BOLTON

Big questions.
Useful work.

I’ve long been drawn to questions about intelligence, power, and how we should live together.

I first explored those questions in political science and philosophy at Furman University. Discovering AI safety in 2016, through Robert Miles’ videos, gave those interests a direction. It led me to pursue a degree in machine learning and AI.

I’m now a staff ML engineer at Boostlingo, leading work on a real-time AI interpreter. I build evaluation systems and help teams use them: setting up meaningful tests, investigating failures, and deciding what evidence is strong enough to ship on.

Before that, I spent a decade building technology at Kid Chess. I moved 4,000 students online during the pandemic, helping the company retain over 90% of its staff and stay afloat. We returned fully to in-person teaching afterward, with the online offering continuing as a new revenue stream. Over my time there, I grew engineering and QA from two to nine. That experience taught me that software only works as well as the people and practices around it.

AI safety is where I’ve wanted this path to lead. I’m especially interested in manipulation, role adherence, and how AI can reinforce power imbalances.

What I’m working toward

I completed BlueDot Impact’s AGI Strategy course in November 2026, earning a certificate for the 30-hour program. Through the course, I’ve been developing an evaluation proposal: when people disagree, do AI notetakers favor the more powerful or fluent speaker? The first step is a related-work review and a proposal, with authority and language fluency tested separately.

I intend to take BlueDot’s Technical AI Safety course next. I’m looking for hands-on engineering roles in behavioral evaluations, adversarial testing, or evaluation infrastructure, with scope and level shaped around the team’s needs.
